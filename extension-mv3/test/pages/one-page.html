A Short Note on Margins

Printers have always left a little white space around the words. It is where readers write, where the eye rests, and where a page tells you who made it and where it came from.

A margin note is the oldest form of reply. Medieval readers argued with their books in the gutters; students still underline a sentence and write a question beside it. None of that needs a battery.

The width of a margin is a small decision with large effects. Too narrow, and the page feels crowded and hurried. Too wide, and it feels like a pamphlet. Most books settle on a measure of about sixty characters a line, with more room at the foot than at the head.

So the next time you print an article, look at the edges before the middle. That quiet frame is doing more work than it seems.
